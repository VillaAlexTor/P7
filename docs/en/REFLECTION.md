> I started p7 wanting to make privacy policies understandable before people accept them. Building it taught me that a convincing AI answer is not enough: I need to show the clause behind every finding and explain what the score means. My first local evaluation exposed a serious mistake: the model treated twelve denials as if the practices happened. After revising the prompt, it recognized the negative cases, but still missed one retention clause. That changed how I think about testing. A working demo proves the pieces connect; a labeled set reveals where they fail. I also learned to keep the Spanish and English experiences aligned and to distinguish declared data flows from observed behavior. If I started again, I would prepare independent evaluation examples and test comprehension with users earlier.
